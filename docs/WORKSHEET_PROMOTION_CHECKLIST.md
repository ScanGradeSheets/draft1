# Worksheet Promotion Checklist

Created 2026-05-30 for the current Grade 2 open-divider worksheet family.

## Purpose

Use this before promoting a worksheet from `test packet` to any stronger status:

- current classroom format
- reusable worksheet library item
- TPT sampler
- paid product page
- public ScanGrade-compatible example

The goal is to keep ScanGrade honest. A worksheet can look good and still be too fragile for students, scanning, or teacher trust.

## Promotion Levels

### Level 0: Test Packet

Meaning:

- Internal/classroom validation only.
- Used to collect handwriting and scan evidence.
- Not marketed as a finished product.

Allowed language:

- "Current test sheets"
- "Open-divider worksheet test"
- "Classroom sample packet"

### Level 1: Classroom-Ready Template

Meaning:

- Tony would hand the format to students again.
- Students generally understand where to write.
- The app can process a small real sample set without total workflow failure.
- Failure modes are explainable.

Allowed language:

- "Current classroom format"
- "Works with the current ScanGrade test workflow"

Avoid:

- public launch claims
- paid product language
- automation promises

### Level 2: Free Sampler / Preview Candidate

Meaning:

- The worksheet works as a normal paper handout.
- Teacher answer key is clean.
- Sample testing supports the basic ScanGrade-compatible story.
- Any scan caveats are known and explainable.

Allowed language:

- "No-prep Grade 2 practice"
- "Large answer boxes"
- "Answer key included"
- "Designed for quick review"

ScanGrade language should still be conservative unless classroom evidence is strong.

### Level 3: Paid Product Candidate

Meaning:

- The format has survived enough classroom evidence to become part of a small product family.
- Preview assets are honest and attractive.
- Terms of use, answer keys, and usage notes are ready.
- Tony has approved product positioning and listing copy.

Allowed language:

- "Grade 2 addition and subtraction practice pack"
- "Clean, no-prep worksheets with answer key"
- "Designed to pair with the ScanGrade workflow"

Still avoid:

- "never mark again"
- "fully automatic grading"
- "guaranteed scanning"
- learning-gain claims

## Gate Checklist

### Student Usability

- [ ] Students know where to write without extra explanation.
- [ ] Most answers stay inside the intended boxes.
- [ ] Center divider marks help or at least do not confuse students.
- [ ] Page feels like normal classwork, not a technical form.
- [ ] Tony would reuse the page format.

### Scan Reliability

- [ ] Full-page photos show visible corner markers.
- [ ] QR/layout identification works on normal photos.
- [ ] Answer crops line up with the handwritten digits.
- [ ] Common classroom imperfections are handled or clearly rejected.
- [ ] Failures have plain-English causes.

### OCR And Trust

- [ ] Clear digits are usually recognized correctly.
- [ ] Unclear digits are flagged or refused rather than confidently guessed.
- [ ] Review-needed answers are easy for a teacher to inspect.
- [ ] Teacher override remains obvious and final.
- [ ] The teacher-trust scorecard has no blocking red issue.

### Product Readiness

- [ ] Student PDF prints cleanly.
- [ ] Combined packet PDF is ready.
- [ ] Teacher answer key is accurate and easy to skim.
- [ ] `ScanGrade.io` is visible but quiet.
- [ ] Preview/cover assets are honest and show the real worksheet.
- [ ] Terms-of-use language exists before public distribution.
- [ ] Listing copy avoids unsupported claims.

## Evidence Needed Before Promotion

Minimum evidence:

- completed real student samples for Sheets A, B, and C
- filled sample evaluation report
- teacher-trust scorecard
- exact commands/results preserved
- Tony's classroom observations

Helpful evidence:

- more than one student per sheet
- at least one imperfect but realistic photo
- notes on whether students understood the answer boxes
- any examples where the app was uncertain instead of wrong

## Default Decision Rule

If evidence is mixed:

```text
Keep the worksheet as a test packet.
```

Do not promote it just because it is visually polished.

Do not reject it just because one photo was poor.

Promote only when the classroom workflow, teacher trust, and product story all line up.
