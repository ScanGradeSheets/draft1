# ScanGrade locked TPT worksheet-first business strategy

Date: 2026-07-18  
Status: Founder-approved and locked  
Decision owner: Tony

## Decision

ScanGrade will launch first as an **auto-grading printable math worksheet brand**.

Teachers buy ScanGrade-authored worksheet packs through Teachers Pay Teachers at prices comparable to other high-quality math worksheet products. They print the worksheets, students complete them on paper, and the teacher scans them at `https://scangrade.io/`. The grader is free and does not require an account or sign-in.

The launch promise is deliberately simple:

> Print it. Students complete it on paper. Scan it. It is graded.

## Launch product boundary

Included:

- Attractive, pedagogically sound ScanGrade-authored worksheet packs.
- QR-linked, declared and auditable answer-zone metadata.
- Free anonymous grading at `scangrade.io`.
- Local-first recognition and teacher review for uncertain handwriting.
- Printable answer keys and clear teacher instructions.
- A free sample that lets teachers test the complete experience before buying.

Not required at launch:

- Software subscriptions or billing on ScanGrade.io.
- Teacher or student accounts.
- Rosters, saved classes, grading history, dashboards, or analytics.
- Arbitrary third-party worksheet recognition.
- Public access to the Mac Mini or a paid cloud-recognition fallback.
- District procurement or integrations.

## Commercial logic

- Teachers already understand and buy printable worksheet products; ScanGrade adds automatic grading without adding a second purchasing decision.
- The worksheet can be priced alongside comparable resources, making the grader a distinctive included benefit rather than a premium technology surcharge.
- The static, browser-local grader has negligible marginal scan cost and avoids early authentication, billing, storage, privacy, and support complexity.
- Every printed sheet carries ScanGrade branding and creates repeat exposure. The free grader is treated as product utility and distribution, not as something that must be access-controlled.
- Future optional paid software features remain available if buyers repeatedly request saved classes, history, analytics, batch workflows, or broader worksheet support.

## Catalog strategy

Start with a small Grade 1 catalog built from the worksheet families that perform reliably and are genuinely useful to teachers. Expand depth before breadth: multiple problem sets, seasonal variants, review packs, intervention packs, assessments, and bundles can reuse a proven family contract.

Grades 2–6 provide a large future catalog opportunity, but expansion is not assumed to be effortless until the production system proves otherwise. New mathematical content can be inexpensive to generate; trustworthy products still require human curriculum judgment and reproducible scan validation.

Every new worksheet family must pass:

1. Curriculum and mathematical QA.
2. Answer-key, question-group, slot-count, and layout-metadata agreement.
3. ScanGrade layout-contract audit.
4. QR destination and public-asset verification.
5. Printable PDF and visual-design QA.
6. Representative phone, tablet, perspective, lighting, and old-device capture tests.
7. Handwritten-truth evaluation with incorrect answers, blanks, erasures, and ambiguous writing represented.
8. Zero known confident transcription errors in its acceptance evidence.
9. Fast, unobscured teacher correction for residual yellow answers.
10. TPT preview, instructions, description, copyright, and customer-support QA.

Once a family passes, producing content variations within the same answer-zone and layout contract should become low marginal effort. A materially new layout family reopens the full validation gate.

## Initial offer structure to test

- Free demonstration pack: 2–3 sheets.
- Individual concept packs: roughly 10–15 sheets, priced with comparable TPT products.
- Unit or skill bundles: roughly 30–50 sheets with a modest bundle discount.
- Later: intervention, assessment, seasonal, cross-concept, and grade-level collections.

Do not lock exact prices until current comparable listings and buyer response are tested. Do not advertise universal handwriting accuracy or arbitrary worksheet support.

## Evidence that validates the strategy

- Teachers can understand the product from the listing and demonstration without founder explanation.
- Buyers successfully scan through the QR workflow without creating an account.
- Refunds and support requests caused by scanning remain low.
- Teachers report meaningful marking-time savings.
- Buyers leave reviews that specifically value automatic grading.
- Free-sample users convert to paid packs.
- Paid buyers make repeat purchases across concepts or bundles.
- Proven worksheet families maintain safe performance across unseen students and ordinary devices.

## Reconsideration triggers

Revisit—but do not silently change—this strategy if:

- Teachers consistently reject ScanGrade-authored formats despite valuing the scanner.
- Free grading does not improve listing conversion, reviews, or repeat purchases.
- Supporting the anonymous grader costs more than worksheet revenue can sustain.
- Buyers repeatedly request saved classes, history, analytics, or broader worksheet support and show willingness to pay.
- A licensing or school opportunity demonstrates materially better economics with acceptable support and privacy obligations.

Any change to the locked launch model requires Tony's explicit approval and an updated decision record.
