# ScanGrade Worksheet Quality Rubric

Created 2026-05-30 to make Tony's worksheet taste easier to capture and apply.

This rubric is internal guidance. It should help Codex evaluate worksheet ideas, preview assets, and TPT products before asking Tony to review them.

It does not authorize changing the current open-divider test packet geometry before classroom evidence arrives.

## Purpose

ScanGrade worksheets need to be good paper worksheets first.

They also need to support scanning, teacher review, brand recognition, and eventual marketplace packaging.

This rubric separates those jobs so a worksheet can be judged clearly instead of vaguely feeling "nice" or "off."

## Scoring Scale

Use a simple 1-4 score for each category:

- 1 = not acceptable
- 2 = usable but weak
- 3 = good enough for classroom use
- 4 = strong enough to represent ScanGrade

Do not average scores blindly. A worksheet with a scan-critical failure is not ready even if it looks good.

## Category 1: Student Clarity

Question:

> Can a Grade 1/2 student immediately understand what to do?

Look for:

- clear title
- obvious question pattern
- clear answer spaces
- no confusing extra marks
- enough spacing for young handwriting

Red flags:

- students might write in the wrong place
- directions are too long
- answer area feels ambiguous
- layout requires adult-like precision

## Category 2: Teacher Usefulness

Question:

> Would Tony actually use this in class without extra explanation or prep?

Look for:

- clean name line
- practical page density
- answer key path
- easy visual scanning while walking around
- useful as paper even without ScanGrade

Red flags:

- teacher has to explain the layout repeatedly
- page feels like a technical form
- answer checking would still be tedious
- worksheet creates more management than practice

## Category 3: Scan Safety

Question:

> Does the worksheet protect the scan/review workflow?

Look for:

- stable corner markers
- QR/layout area preserved
- answer boxes match known layout
- no decorative shapes that resemble markers/digits
- high contrast for pencil marks
- full page remains photographable

Red flags:

- answer boxes moved without layout update
- markers or QR crowded by decoration
- lines compete with handwriting
- student may write outside the crop
- scan-critical geometry changed without evidence

## Category 4: Visual Calm

Question:

> Does the page feel clean and trustworthy rather than busy?

Look for:

- enough white space
- consistent alignment
- quiet gray where helpful
- strong but not oversized title
- repeated structure
- no decorative filler

Red flags:

- too many type sizes
- heavy borders
- clipart for decoration
- cluttered footer/header
- one-note brand color overuse

## Category 5: Grade Fit

Question:

> Does this fit real Grade 1/2 handwriting and classroom behavior?

Look for:

- large answer areas
- tolerance for uneven pencil marks
- room for reversed or imperfect digits
- short routines
- minimal reading burden

Red flags:

- tiny boxes
- narrow writing lines
- too many questions for the skill/routine
- pages that require neat adult spacing

## Category 6: Product Identity

Question:

> Does this feel like a ScanGrade worksheet rather than generic printable content?

Look for:

- consistent answer-box language
- quiet `ScanGrade.io` visibility
- clean modern Scantron feel
- purposeful scan-friendly structure
- premium but classroom-practical presentation

Red flags:

- brand is invisible
- brand overwhelms the student task
- worksheet could be any generic worksheet
- design relies on decoration instead of structure

## Category 7: Marketplace Readiness

Question:

> Could this be part of a product a teacher understands quickly?

Look for:

- clear skill focus
- understandable title
- answer key included
- preview-friendly layout
- repeatable family format
- no unsupported claims

Red flags:

- product promise depends on unproven scan behavior
- worksheet is one-off rather than scalable
- preview would need heavy explanation
- broad curriculum claims without evidence

## Automatic Rejects

Reject or revise immediately if:

- students cannot tell where to answer
- answer boxes do not match the layout definition
- QR or markers are compromised
- page is decorative before it is useful
- `ScanGrade.io` is removed without Tony approval
- app/scanning claims exceed classroom evidence
- a new format is introduced before the current format is validated

## Tony Review Prompts

When showing Tony worksheet options, ask focused questions:

1. Would you hand this to your class tomorrow?
2. Where would students get confused?
3. Does this feel too plain, too busy, or about right?
4. Are the answer boxes big enough?
5. Does the brand feel visible but not distracting?
6. Would you sell this, use it, both, or neither?
7. What would make you reject it instantly?

## Codex Default Recommendation

Until Tony gives deeper worksheet-taste guidance, Codex should prefer:

- 10 questions per page for the current Grade 2 math format
- mostly black/white/quiet gray student pages
- no decorative clipart
- Lexend typography
- open-divider answer boxes
- small visible `ScanGrade.io`
- answer key included
- product preview assets that show the actual page clearly
